Contributor Catalyst is a program designed to introduce HBCU undergraduates and recent graduates to open-source software, collaborative development, and the communities behind technology. Through hands-on projects, mentorship, and teamwork, participants gain real-world experience while learning how to contribute to meaningful open-source projects.
The 2026 cohort had the opportunity to work together, develop technical skills, and build connections with others who shared similar interests. Throughout the program, participants learned how to use tools such as GitHub, create branches and pull requests, test projects locally, and communicate effectively within a team. One of our projects involved improving the offline reference for p5.js, helping make its educational resources more accessible to people without an internet connection.

As part of the program, the cohort traveled to Santa Cruz, where we connected in person, shared our progress, and celebrated everything we accomplished. We also toured GitHub headquarters and learned more about how its employees work. In addition, we were invited to visit Bloomberg, where we saw how the company operates and learned what open source means within its field. These experiences gave us a better understanding of how the skills we were developing could be used in professional environments.

The trip was not only about technology and professional development. We also went on little adventures and enjoyed spending time together outside of work. We went hiking, visited the Mystery Spot, and attended a bonfire, which was some of ourmy first times experiencing one. We even had a friendly competition over Pizza My Heart versus Woodstock’s Pizza. I think we can finally agree that Pizza My Heart won!
These moments brought out the human side of the program. Contributor Catalyst gave us the experience of following a professional work schedule. Our core working hours were from 1p.m to 5 p.m., with additional asynchronous work completed outside those hours. Even with our responsibilities, we still had time to enjoy life, try new things, and build meaningful relationships with the people around us. The program helped us develop our technical skills, confidence, problem-solving abilities, leadership skills, and sense of community. Contributor Catalyst felt like more than just a technology program it felt like a family.



#Jordin’s take

My journey with Contributor Catalyst has been especially meaningful because I first joined the program as a participant and later returned in mentorship roles. As a participant, I learned how open-source communities work and became more comfortable asking questions, sharing ideas, and contributing code. Returning as a mentor allowed me to use those experiences to guide others through challenges that I once faced myself.
I remember feeling nervous and confused when Emily first began teaching me. However, after replaying her explanations and listening closely, I realized that there was not much to be nervous about. Almost anything can be learned and improved with enough patience and practice. Remembering how I felt as a participant helped me become a more understanding and supportive mentor.
Mentoring the 2026 cohort helped me continue developing as both a leader and a technologist. I enjoyed watching participants become more confident, work through technical problems, and recognize the value of their contributions. This experience reminded me that mentorship is not only about providing answers. It is also about encouraging people, helping them discover their strengths, and creating an environment where everyone feels comfortable learning.
Contributor Catalyst has played an important role in my personal and professional growth. It has given me technical experience, meaningful connections, and opportunities to support the next group of open-source contributors. I am grateful that I was able to return as a mentor and give back to a program that has had such a positive impact on my journey.

#Silas’ Take

Working with Contributor Catalyst this year has been such an eye-opening growth experience. I had the opportunity to lead the program for a week while Emily, the program lead, was away. Through a great deal of preparation and on-the-spot improvisation, I affirmed the confidence I held in my ability to teach, mentor, and leave a positive impact on people’s lives. 

This year I paid close attention to how individuals progressed from the beginning of the program to the end. It was really cool to see how everyone went from asking for help as soon as they hit a road bump to relying on their peers for solutions and doing investigations on their own. I know open source isn’t for everyone, so it was cool to see a group where everyone found their own niche and showed passion for the issues we were working on. What I saw most was a general passion for problem-solving and the joy of finding solutions. 

I’m especially grateful for the group of participants I got to know this year. Melissa from the office of ABC Student Success coined the term “Catalyst Community”, and I 100% felt that this year. The way the housing was set up felt like we had our own small community in the neck of the woods. We got to see how each other lived, and pretty much had an open-door policy. We had movie nights, game nights, hosted a birthday party, and found so many ways to connect.

We had so many in-town excursions: we went to get food, go thrifting, and see movies. I went to the bookstore maybe twice a week, and someone was always eager to join me. We also saw The Odyssey, and a handful of us bought the novel and some other books that we started reading at a book club.

It was really such a heartwarming experience, and I learned so much about everything life entails from the new connections I made. It really felt more like a family than a group of coworkers, and I’m grateful for every interaction and conversation I had.
